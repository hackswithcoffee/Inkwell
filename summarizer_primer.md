# D&D 5E Quick Reference — Inkwell Summarizer Primer

This primer exists to help you correctly INTERPRET events in the raw transcript.
Use it to disambiguate game terminology. Do NOT include raw mechanics
(dice rolls, AC, HP totals, save DCs) in the chronicle prose itself.

---

## Dying & Death — the single most common misinterpretation

When a character drops to 0 Hit Points (HP):

- They fall **UNCONSCIOUS**. They are **NOT DEAD**.
- They begin rolling **DEATH SAVING THROWS** (a.k.a. "death saves") on each of their turns.
- Each death save is a d20 roll: 10+ is a success, 9 or lower is a failure. A natural 1 counts as 2 failures; a natural 20 instantly returns the character to 1 HP and consciousness.

Outcomes:

- **3 successes** → the character is **STABLE**: still unconscious, but no longer dying. Wakes with 1 HP after 1d4 hours, sooner if healed.
- **3 failures** → the character **DIES**.
- Any **healing** received while at 0 HP → conscious again, back in the fight.
- An ally's **DC 10 Medicine check** to stabilize → also Stable, no more death saves.

### Transcript phrases and what they ACTUALLY mean

| Phrase                                                | Means                              |
|-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| "he's down", "she dropped", "I'm at zero"             | Unconscious, NOT dead              |
| "rolling death saves", "made my death save"           | Still alive, dying                 |
| "failed one", "got two failures"                      | Still alive (death is at 3)        |
| "stabilized", "I stabilize them"                      | Still unconscious but no longer dying |
| "healing word on him", "potion of healing", "I revivify" | Back up, conscious, fighting   |
| "three failures", "he's gone", DM says "X is dead", players start discussing a replacement character | ACTUALLY dead |

**Default assumption when a PC drops:** wounded / dying / unconscious — never slain. Only describe a death when one of the unambiguous markers above appears.

### Approved vocabulary for downed-but-alive characters

"struck down", "fallen", "lying still", "teetering at death's door",
"clinging to life", "gravely wounded", "lay dying", "knocked senseless",
"crumpled to the ground". Reserve "slain", "killed", "fell forever",
"perished" for confirmed deaths only.

## Healing Potions — preserve the tier

These are DIFFERENT items. Never collapse them.

| Item                         | Heals        | Rarity    |
|------------------------------|--------------|-----------|
| Potion of Healing            | 2d4 + 2 HP   | Common    |
| Potion of Greater Healing    | 4d4 + 4 HP   | Uncommon  |
| Potion of Superior Healing   | 8d4 + 8 HP   | Rare      |
| Potion of Supreme Healing    | 10d4 + 20 HP | Very Rare |

If the transcript references "a greater healing", that's a **Potion of Greater Healing** — record it as such, not as "Potion of Healing".

## Magic Item Rarities

Common < Uncommon < Rare < Very Rare < Legendary < Artifact.

Magic weapons and armor often carry a plus tier: +1, +2, +3. A
"+1 longsword" and "+2 longsword" are DIFFERENT items — always preserve the number.

## Currency

Coins, smallest to largest:

`cp` (copper) < `sp` (silver) < `ep` (electrum) < `gp` (gold) < `pp` (platinum)

Conversions: 10 cp = 1 sp · 5 sp = 1 ep · 2 ep = 1 gp · 10 gp = 1 pp.

"50 gold" = 50 gp. Record currency precisely (amount + denomination).

## Rests

- **Short rest** — about 1 hour. The party catches breath, spends Hit Dice for partial healing, recovers some abilities. Often taken between encounters.
- **Long rest** — about 8 hours (sleep + watch). Full HP, most spell slots and class abilities restored. Marks the end of an adventuring day.

## Key Conditions — do not conflate

| Condition     | Meaning (briefly)                                                |
|---------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Unconscious   | Out cold; can't act. ≠ DEAD.                                     |
| Stable        | 0 HP but not dying. Still unconscious.                           |
| Paralyzed     | Frozen in place but conscious.                                   |
| Stunned       | Disoriented; can't take actions briefly.                         |
| Petrified     | Turned to stone.                                                 |
| Charmed       | Magically friendly to a target.                                  |
| Frightened    | Can't approach the source of fear.                               |
| Poisoned      | Sickened; weakened attacks and checks.                           |
| Prone         | Knocked down, on the ground.                                     |
| Grappled      | Held in place by a foe.                                          |
| Restrained    | Bound or pinned; can't move freely.                              |
| Invisible     | Can't be seen.                                                   |
| Blinded / Deafened | Can't see / can't hear.                                     |
| Exhaustion    | Levels 1–6; level 6 = death.                                     |

## Combat Terms — for your understanding only, NOT for the prose

- **Saving throw / save** — a defensive roll against some effect (Strength save, Dexterity save, Constitution save, etc.). NOT the same as a death save.
- **Skill check** — Perception, Persuasion, Stealth, Investigation, Athletics, Insight, etc.
- **AC** (Armor Class) — how hard a target is to hit.
- **HP** (Hit Points) — health.
- **Initiative** — turn order in combat.
- **Advantage / Disadvantage** — roll twice; take the better / worse.
- **Critical hit** — a natural 20 on the attack roll.

## Spell Levels & Common Named Spells

Spells have levels 0–9. Level 0 are **cantrips** (castable at will). Higher-level spells consume spell slots.

Common named spells in transcripts — preserve the exact spell name:

- **Healing & life:** Cure Wounds, Healing Word, Mass Cure Wounds, Spare the Dying, Revivify, Raise Dead, Resurrection.
- **Cantrips:** Sacred Flame, Fire Bolt, Eldritch Blast, Vicious Mockery, Toll the Dead, Guidance, Light, Mage Hand.
- **Damage:** Fireball, Lightning Bolt, Magic Missile, Burning Hands, Scorching Ray, Cone of Cold.
- **Control:** Hold Person, Hypnotic Pattern, Sleep, Polymorph, Banishment, Wall of Fire, Web.
- **Utility:** Counterspell, Shield, Misty Step, Fly, Invisibility, Dispel Magic.
- **Buffs/debuffs:** Bless, Bane, Haste, Slow, Faerie Fire.

## Class roles

When a character's class is named in the transcript (or implied by their actions),
use these archetypes to interpret what they "do." Don't invent class details —
only describe what the transcript actually shows.

- **Cleric / Paladin** — divine casters; often heal and buff allies.
- **Wizard / Sorcerer / Warlock** — arcane casters; ranged spell damage and control.
- **Fighter / Barbarian / Monk** — frontline melee combatants.
- **Rogue / Ranger** — skirmishers; sneak attacks, ranged combat, scouting.
- **Druid / Bard** — nature / charisma casters; versatile support and utility.

## Items: Purchased vs Found

When recording acquired items, you MUST distinguish:

- **Purchased** — bought from a shop, merchant, or vendor in exchange for gold or barter.
- **Found** — looted from a defeated foe, recovered from a chest / dungeon / body, awarded as a quest reward, or otherwise acquired in the field.

Always prefix entries with an exact count: "3x Potion of Healing",
"1x +1 Longsword", "250 gp". If quantity is not stated, write "1x"
(never "a few", "some", "several").
